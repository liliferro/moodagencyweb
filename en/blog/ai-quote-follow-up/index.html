AI-assisted quote follow-up: a 7-touch sequence

What to say and when after sending a quote. Seven touchpoints, each with a different purpose, ready to adapt.

Automation starts with a process someone can explain. If your team doesn't know who should handle an inquiry, connecting more tools will only spread the confusion faster. A good first project is focused, repeatable and easy to oversee.

Map your current process

Map each step from the moment an inquiry arrives to the first response. Note which systems are involved, what the person responsible needs to know and where delays tend to occur. Include exceptions: incomplete data, duplicates, out-of-scope requests and existing contacts.

Choose a workflow with a clear outcome, such as creating a task to review a new inquiry. Avoid starting with a lengthy email and messaging sequence before you've decided who should respond.

Separate triggers, rules and actions

A trigger should represent a confirmed event. Rules determine which path to follow. Actions update a system or notify someone. Documenting these three elements lets you review the workflow with your team without relying on a particular tool's interface.

For example: after receiving a valid form submission, check whether the contact already exists. If so, update the relevant opportunity; otherwise, create a record and assign it for review. Agree on matching rules and which information can be updated before you start.

Plan for failures, too

Define what happens if the CRM stops responding, a permission expires or a field is missing. A workflow may need retries, a review queue or an alert. The owner should be able to see what was processed and what remains pending without manually checking every application.

Make sure retries don't create multiple contacts or send duplicate messages. A stable operation ID helps identify actions that have already run. Keep only the logs needed to investigate errors and restrict who can access them.

Attribution needs real fields

Tally can pass website parameters into forms and store them in configured hidden fields. Adding a UTM parameter to a URL doesn't prove the form is capturing it. Check the resulting record and confirm it reaches its destination, respecting privacy preferences.

How to assess your pilot

Compare response times, pending records, duplicates and errors before and after implementation. Review a sample of operations with your team. If a workflow reduces tasks but creates exceptions that are hard to resolve, it still needs work.

A useful automation has an owner, documentation and a way to stop it. Expand its scope when the team can understand and maintain it, not just when a demo works.

Practical template: following up on quotes

Sending a quote without following up leaves the sale to chance. One approach is to plan up to seven touchpoints over a month, each with a different purpose. This is a template to adapt, not a required frequency or a proven formula for every customer. AI drafts the messages; you review and send them.

A common mistake is sending 'just following up' three times. That gives your customer no reason to respond.

The sequence

DayChannelPurpose
0EmailSend the quote with a three-line summary: what's included, the price and the delivery date
2WhatsApp or phoneConfirm receipt and ask whether they need anything else to make a decision
5EmailAddress the most common question about this kind of purchase before they ask
9EmailShare a relevant customer story and its outcome
14PhoneAsk about the buying process: who else is involved and what is the timeline?
21EmailOffer an alternative: a different format, order volume or timeline
30EmailClose the loop politely: 'Would it be better to revisit this later?' Give your customer an easy way out and yourself a clear answer

The timing is a guide. Space contacts further apart for major purchases; bring them closer together for urgent ones.

How to use AI at each touchpoint

Give your assistant the quote, the customer's industry and the purpose of the message, then ask for a draft under 80 words. For example:

'I sent this quote five days ago to the purchasing manager at a food company: [summary]. Write a short email addressing the most common delivery-time question. Avoid salesy language.'

Then schedule reminders in your CRM so no follow-up relies on anyone's memory. If you aren't using one yet, read how to choose a CRM for an industrial business.

Three rules

  • Every message should offer something new.
  • If the customer replies, stop the sequence and have a person take over.
  • If they ask you to stop contacting them, respect that immediately.

Try this today

  • List the open quotes from the last 30 days
  • Check how many received fewer than three follow-ups
  • Write the day-five message for the most important one

Frequently asked questions

Isn't seven touchpoints too many?

It can be. Agree on frequency, consider the channel and stop the sequence after a reply, rejection or request not to be contacted.

Can everything be automated?

Reminders and drafts can. A person should review messages before they are sent, especially for major accounts.
