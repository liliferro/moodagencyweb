20 AI prompts for B2B prospecting

Ready-to-use prompts for defining your ideal customer, researching companies, writing your first message and preparing a call.

By Mood Agency · Published on November 14, 2026

In this guide

  • Research a company
  • Write the first message
  • Follow up
  • Prepare the call
  • Learn from what happened
  • What not to ask for

An AI assistant doesn't do prospecting for you, but it can help with research, summaries and drafts. Time savings depend on the task and the review involved. These 20 prompts cover the whole process, from defining your target to preparing the first call. Copy them and replace the text in brackets.

One rule before you start: give context. Explain what you sell, to whom and what makes it different. The more specific you are, the better the response.

Define who to look for

  1. 'These are my five best customers: [list with industry, size and city]. What do they have in common? Describe my ideal customer in five lines.'
  2. 'I sell [product] to [type of company]. Which other industries might need it, and why?'
  3. 'Which roles usually take part in purchasing [product] at a [industry] company? Explain what matters to each.'
  4. 'What signals suggest a [industry] company may soon need [product]?'

Research a company

  1. 'Summarize what this company does in five lines using its website text: [paste text].'
  2. 'Based on this company information [paste], give me three reasons it might need [product].'
  3. 'Rank these companies by how closely they match my ideal customer, and explain why: [list].'
  4. 'What should I ask [role] at a [industry] company to understand whether I can help?'

Write the first message

  1. 'Write an email under 90 words to [role] at [company]. Open with this detail about their business: [fact]. No adjectives or sales jargon. End with a question.'
  2. 'Give me three subject lines under six words for that email, with no exclamation marks.'
  3. 'Rewrite this email so it sounds like a person rather than a template: [email].'
  4. 'Write a LinkedIn connection note under 250 characters for [role], mentioning [fact].'

Follow up

  1. 'I sent this quote a week ago and haven't heard back: [summary]. Write a short follow-up that adds something new.'
  2. 'Give me five different reasons to contact a prospect again without repeating “just following up.”'
  3. 'Summarize this customer conversation in three points and identify the next step: [notes].'

Prepare the call

  1. 'I'm meeting [role] at a [industry] company. Give me the five most likely objections and how to address each.'
  2. 'Prepare a 30-minute agenda for a first sales meeting with [company].'
  3. 'Explain the [customer's industry] process in plain language so I can arrive prepared.'

Learn from what happened

  1. 'Here are notes from my last ten calls: [notes]. Which objections keep coming up?'
  2. 'What do these prospects who didn't buy have in common? [list].'

What not to ask for

Don't ask it for company lists or contact details: it may invent them or provide incomplete information. Use a directory instead. We explain how to find companies with DENUE. And don't paste confidential customer information into a personal account.
