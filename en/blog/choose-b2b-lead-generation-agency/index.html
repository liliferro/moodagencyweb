How to choose a B2B lead generation agency

Ten questions to ask an agency before signing, what to look for in each answer and the warning signs to watch.

By Mood Agency · Published on November 14, 2026

In this guide
  • The 10 questions
  • Warning signs
  • What to prepare before the meeting
  • Try this today
  • Frequently asked questions

Before hiring a lead generation agency, ask ten questions. The answers will tell you whether it understands B2B sales or simply knows how to drive traffic. A good agency answers directly—including what it can't promise.

The 10 questions

#QuestionWhat to expect
1Have you worked with businesses that sell to other businesses?Specific case studies, not just consumer brands
2What do you consider a qualified lead?A definition agreed with you, not just 'a completed form'
3Where will the leads come from?Specific channels and a reason for choosing each
4What happens after a lead comes in?Follow-up and integration with your CRM
5How will you measure results?Opportunities and cost per opportunity, not just clicks
6What do you need from my team?Sales team time, access and information. Be wary if they say 'nothing'
7When will I see results?Timelines that vary by channel, without first-week promises
8What can't you guarantee?Closed sales and search rankings
9Who owns the data and accounts?You do: ad accounts, CRM, lists and content
10How do you use AI, and who oversees it?Specific use cases with human review

Warning signs

  • They guarantee a number of sales.
  • They charge only per lead, regardless of quality.
  • They don't ask about your sales process.
  • They report visits and followers as results.
  • Ad accounts remain in their name.

What to prepare before the meeting

Bring three figures: your average customer value, typical sales cycle and how many of every ten quotes you close. These help assess a proposal. Sales capacity, margins, coverage and data quality also matter.

Frequently asked questions

Should we hire an agency or build an in-house team?

An agency makes sense when you lack time or specialist skills; an in-house team when volume justifies it. Many businesses start with an agency and later bring the work in-house.

How long should a reasonable contract last?

Long enough to observe a full sales cycle. In industrial sales, that often means several months.

Can I start on my own?

Yes. Follow the method for finding companies or start with a small budget.
