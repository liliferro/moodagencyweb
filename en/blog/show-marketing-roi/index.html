How to show leadership the return on marketing

Which numbers to bring to your leadership team, how to connect them to sales and a one-page report you can create this month.

By Mood Agency · Published on November 14, 2026

To assess marketing's contribution to sales, leadership needs more than visits and followers. They want to know how many sales opportunities marketing generated and what each one cost. Answer those two questions on one page and the budget conversation changes.

The four numbers that matter

MetricHow to calculate it
Qualified leadsContacts that meet the criteria agreed with sales
OpportunitiesDeals accepted by sales under an agreed definition; not every quote is a sales opportunity
Cost per opportunityMarketing investment for the period divided by the number of opportunities
Marketing-sourced salesClosed revenue from those opportunities

Everything else—visits, clicks and reach—helps explain changes in these four numbers, but doesn't belong on the first page.

Three requirements for measurement

  1. A definition of a qualified lead agreed with sales. Without one, each team counts differently. Here's how to define it.
  2. Know where each lead came from. Save the source on every contact and always ask 'How did you hear about us?' Many customers come through referrals or AI assistants. Self-reported sources complement technical attribution, which may be incomplete.
  3. A CRM where sales records what happened. Without that feedback, marketing never knows which lead became a customer.

To set up measurement, read how to measure leads without confusing clicks with customers.

How to calculate return without confusing it with revenue

One operational ROMI formula is: (contribution margin attributable to marketing − total marketing cost) / total marketing cost × 100. Use the same period or cohort and explain your attribution model. Contribution margin should deduct variable sales costs without deducting marketing costs a second time.

Hypothetical example: 80,000 in attributable contribution margin and 50,000 in marketing costs produce a 60% ROMI. If you only know revenue, don't present it as profitability. If attribution isn't defensible, disclose the limitation and report observed opportunities.

The one-page report

  • Top: the four metrics, this month versus last month.
  • Middle: the source channel for each opportunity.
  • Bottom: what you learned and what you'll change.

AI can help assemble the report. Give an approved tool an aggregated CRM export with no personal data and ask for a summary and the three main observations. Check the numbers before presenting them.

Be clear about timelines

Some industrial sales take months to close. Explain your business's observed sales cycle and distinguish current-period results from sales that originated in earlier periods. Promising immediate sales is a quick way to lose credibility.

Try this today

  • Ask sales for this month's quotes and identify those sourced by marketing
  • Calculate your cost per opportunity, even if it's an estimate
  • Add 'How did you hear about us?' to your form

Frequently asked questions

What if I don't have a CRM?

Start with a spreadsheet shared with sales. What matters is recording the source and outcome.

How often should we report?

Monthly for the four metrics; quarterly for closed sales.
